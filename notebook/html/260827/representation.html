Complex representation analysis

Following Figure 6 of the co-folding representation study (Boltz2-as-FM, arXiv:2602.13249): how does our pretrained density-ViT encoder relate to other protein–ligand complex encoders? We test four angles on the same 1320 LP-PDBbind (lp_edrscc_v2) test complexes: (1) how aligned the representation spaces are, (2) whether they are complementary for affinity, (3) whether our density-conditioned encoder carries richer information — its representation reconstructs and stitches into the others while resisting the reverse — and (4) label-light quality / information metrics from recent ML work (effective rank, intrinsic dimension, 𝒱-usable information).

Key findings
  • Distinct space (§1). Our two density encoders are each other's closest match (CKA 0.58) yet sit apart from every external complex encoder (CKA 0.07–0.20) — a distinct representation region.
  • Helps affinity (§2). On the canonical train→test probe density gains +0.054 ρ (C+D+G vs matched coords). The within-test CV diagnostic compresses this and is used only for the cross-encoder ensemble comparison (best: IPNet Δρ +0.036).
  • Richer, and quantified (§3). Against a same-recipe seed-twin ceiling, another CDG recovers 100% of it but coordinates recover only 67% — so ~33% of CDG's information is density-unique (unreconstructable from coordinates).
  • Information & geometry (§4). Density lifts effective rank (163→178) and intrinsic dimension (8.9→12.0); it adds usable affinity information on 58% of complexes.
  • Takeaway. Electron density yields a richer, higher-dimensional, distinct complex representation, and its richness converts into a solid downstream gain (canonical affinity +0.054 ρ, plus generation).
Structure — §1 distinct space · §2 converts to affinity (+0.054 ρ) · §3 richer, quantified (~33% density-unique) · §4 corroborating metrics.

3Information richness: cross-representation decodability & stitching

Does one encoder's representation contain another's? Following model stitching (Bansal et al., NeurIPS 2021; Lenc & Vedaldi, CVPR 2015) and its "more-is-better" asymmetry, we fit a low-capacity 2-layer MLP connector to reconstruct each target representation from each source (held-out 5-fold R²). A representation is richer if it reconstructs others well yet is itself hard to reconstruct. We then ground this in the affinity task (functional stitching), and add a training-free check via relative representations (Moschella et al., ICLR 2023 Oral).

3.1Reconstruction R² & richness ranking

Held-out variance-weighted R² of a 2-layer MLP mapping source (row) → target (column). A rich source reconstructs others (high row) while resisting reconstruction (low column). The richness score = meanj[R²(i→j) − R²(j→i)] ranks encoders by net subsumption; higher = contains more of the others' information. (This aggregate score is confounded by target dimensionality — §3.4 gives a ceiling-normalized, confound-free quantification of the density-unique information.)

3.3Relative-representation zero-shot stitching

Training-free (Moschella et al., ICLR 2023): re-express every complex by its cosine similarity to 256 shared anchor complexes, then train an affinity head on CDG's relative rep and apply it zero-shot to each encoder's relative rep (same anchors → shared coordinates). High sim-to-CDG = near-isometric to CDG; a large drop from CDG's own zero-shot ρ means the space does not communicate with CDG (distinct information).

3.4Seed-twin ceiling: density-unique information

Raw reconstruction R² confounds genuinely unique information with mere target complexity. To divide that out we set a ceiling = reconstruction R² between two CDG models that differ only in random seed (same 100M recipe) — the best achievable when the information is identical (ceiling R² = 0.926). Normalized recovery = R²(X→CDG) / ceiling is the fraction of CDG's recoverable information captured by source X; 1 − normalized is what X cannot reconstruct. For coords-only this residual is exactly the density-unique information: 33% of CDG's reconstructable information is not present in coordinates (an independently-seeded CDG recovers the ceiling, another CDG config recovers near it, coords falls well short).

  • Reading richness: CDG reconstructs coords-only C better than C reconstructs CDG (positive asymmetry) ⇒ the density channels add information not recoverable from coordinates. The richness bar ranks all five encoders by net decodability asymmetry.

Normalizing by it removes the target-complexity confound so the coords residual is a clean estimate of density-unique information.

4.1Effective rank, intrinsic dimension, task rank

RankMe (Garrido et al., ICML 2023) = exp(entropy of singular values): over how many dimensions variance spreads (label-free richness). TwoNN intrinsic dimension (Valeriani et al., NeurIPS 2023). LiDAR (Thilak et al., ICLR 2024, adapted with pK-quantile classes) = effective rank of the affinity-discriminative directions. Caveat: RankMe/ID are bounded by feature dimension, so read our C vs C+D+G (both 640-d) directly; cross-encoder values are only indicative.

4.2𝒱-usable information about affinity

Predictive 𝒱-information (Ethayarajh et al., ICML 2022; Xu et al., ICLR 2020) = ½·ln(Var(pK)/MSE) — the usable information a representation carries about pK. On this within-test CV diagnostic C+D+G carries +0.022 nats over coords; on the canonical train→test probe (§2) the gain is +0.027 nats (+0.054 ρ) — nats compress the magnitude, since density improves ranking (ρ) more than MSE. Pointwise, density adds usable information on 58% of complexes.

4.3Platonic convergence

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., ICML 2024) argues strong models converge to a shared representation (measured by the mutual-k-NN / CKNNA alignment used in §1). Mean CKA to the other encoders quantifies how "on-manifold" each encoder is: the external pretrained encoders cluster, while our density encoder sits off it. Distant ≠ rich, though — an encoder can be off-manifold because it is idiosyncratic or weak rather than richly complementary; §3–§4 (richness, information) are what separate our distinct-and-rich from a merely distant encoder.

  • Cross-encoder caveat: RankMe and intrinsic dimension are capped by feature dimensionality (IPNet 256-d, ProFSA / BindNet 1024-d), so the same-dimension C vs C+D+G contrast is the controlled comparison; 𝒱-info and LiDAR are dimension-robust (fixed target / bounded by #classes).
