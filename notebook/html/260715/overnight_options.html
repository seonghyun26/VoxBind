2 · Gaussian-splatting decoding — feasible & well-precedented

Short answer: yes. The forward pass is already Gaussian splatting — the voxelizer splats each atom as a Gaussian (scatter_add, σ = radius/res). A splatting decoder is the inverse: fit Gaussians to the generated density.

Current decoder (the baseline to beat). VoxBind generates a density grid, then decodes atoms by peak-finding: threshold (>0.25) → per-channel 3D local-maxima (voxelizer.find_peaks/get_atom_coords). It's discrete (atoms snap to the 0.25 Å voxel grid), non-differentiable, per-channel, and models no atom shape — just "is this voxel a local max?". There is a refine=True hook in the voxelizer but the production path is peak-finding.

Prior work (this is a trodden path)

  • VoxMol / "denoising voxel grids" (Pinheiro 2023, the direct VoxBind ancestor) already fits atoms by differentiable re-voxelization + L-BFGS, minimizing L2 between re-splatted and generated density — this is splatting-fit decoding.
  • DiffFit (IEEE TVCG 2024) — differentiable fitting of atom structures into cryo-EM density by sampling density at atom coords.
  • TEMPy-ReFF — atoms as a Gaussian-mixture; per-atom variance = B-factor (exactly our density+∇ρ story).
  • 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) — anisotropic Gaussian primitives, opacity, differentiable rasterization, densify/prune for variable count.
  • Atlas Gaussians Diffusion (2024) — generating 3D content as sets of Gaussians via diffusion.

Options ladder (cheap → ambitious)

Opt 0 · L-BFGS re-splat refinement effort: low recommended first
Keep peak-finding for init (atom count + rough positions), then optimize atom (x,y,z) [and optionally per-atom σ/amplitude] by re-voxelizing (splatting) and minimizing L2 to the generated density via L-BFGS/Adam. Sub-voxel accuracy, better bond geometry & validity, fully differentiable. This is the proven VoxMol recipe and may partly exist behind refine=. Fastest path to a decode-quality win; low risk. Measure on the existing Vina/QED/validity harness.
Opt 1 · Anisotropic Gaussians (3DGS-style) effort: medium
Give each atom a full covariance Σ (not just spherical σ) + amplitude, fit differentiably. Captures bonded/anisotropic density and matches the density+∇ρ channels better than isotropic blobs. Natural extension of Opt 0.
Opt 2 · Learned splatting-decoder head effort: high (research)
Replace the heuristic peak-finder with a neural decoder that emits a SET of Gaussians (μ, Σ, atom-type, opacity) from the latent/density, trained end-to-end through a differentiable splatting rasterizer (à la 3DGS). Permutation-invariant, handles variable atom count via opacity prune/densify, differentiable throughout. This is the real research bet — and the density encoder from this campaign is a natural conditioner/initializer for it.
Opt 3 · Generate-as-Gaussians (reframe) effort: high (new model)
Generate the molecule directly as a sparse set of 3D Gaussians instead of a dense voxel grid — a hybrid of point-cloud and voxel diffusion. No voxel-resolution ceiling, continuous coordinates, differentiable end-to-end. Biggest upside, biggest change; a genuine new model rather than a decoder swap.
Recommendation. Start with Opt 0 (a day of work, high precedent, likely improves bond geometry / validity on the current generations) to de-risk and get a measurable win. Then Opt 2 as the research direction, reusing the density encoder. Opt 3 is the moonshot if the splatting decoder proves out. All four are compatible with the existing walk-jump sampler — they only change the density → atoms step, not generation.
