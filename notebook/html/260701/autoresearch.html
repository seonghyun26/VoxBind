Diverse atoms help the role rep (+0.031: 0.554→0.585) — the dropped metals/halogens carry real signal. The D+G-grouping win transfers: [1,1,2] = 0.592 (+0.007 over [1,1,1,1] — the same +0.007 it gave the atomblob), so grouping density+gradmag is a representation-agnostic improvement. But atom-bias does NOT transfer — it hurt the role rep (−0.031 → 0.561): concentrating the mask on the few atom blobs destroys too much in a 2-channel rep (it only helped the redundant 11-channel atomblob). Arm concluded: role-split caps at [1,1,2] 0.592 < atomblob 0.637 — the gap is element-identity that role+size can't recover, not a tuning deficit. The on-theme frontier test (diverse atoms as expanded element channels) needs a vocab rebuild (element vocab is hardcoded 7-lig/4-poc) — flagged for approval, not a config override.

Data-scale verdict: 6.5× more pretrain data does NOT help. Re-running the best role config [1,1,2] on the full PLINDER-v2 corpus (113K complexes, 6.5× the 17.5K) gives ρ 0.586 — flat vs the 17K run's 0.592 (Δ −0.006, within seed noise), despite ~3.3× more total training samples. Data quantity is not the bottleneck — the plateau is representation/capacity-bound, not data-starved. This predicts the pending element-channel [8,4,2]-on-v2 run will win (if at all) from its per-element representation, not from the larger corpus.

Phase 5 — pretext objective (SSL signal)

The whole campaign used masked-reconstruction MAE. Does a different self-supervised objective read density better? Denoising = no masking; corrupt the density+‖∇ρ‖ channels with Gaussian noise and reconstruct the clean field — a density-denoising autoencoder that directly targets density-reading. Frontier follow-up on the [7,4,2] winner.

ObjectiveWhat it reconstructsρ (lp_edrscc_v2)rRMSE
masked reconstruction (MAE) refmask 50% holes → inpaint0.637 ±.0060.656 ±.0061.353 ±.031
density denoising AEno mask; density+∇ρ +N(0,1)·0.5 → reconstruct clean0.587 ±.0050.605 ±.0131.519 ±.122
Denoising HURT (0.587, −0.050). Same lesson as masking: the masked-reconstruction task forces the encoder to infer missing structure from context (harder, more semantic → better features), whereas denoise-in-place is a local low-level task. MAE wins; the pretext axis is settled. (electra — the other pretext — isn't applicable to 13-channel C+D+G.)

The apparent winner was seed luck. Across two pretrain seeds the two-seed means are base 0.633 ≥ atom-bias 0.630 — atom-bias is not better, and it's far less stable (spread 0.022 vs the base's 0.007). Its 0.641 was a single-seed high; seed 1 (0.619) is below the base. So nothing reliably beats [7,4,2] ≈ 0.63. The honest reading of the whole campaign: most Phase-3 trial-to-trial differences sit within this pretrain-seed noise; only the large effects exceed it — grouping over coords (real gain), and the clear regressions (capacity, denoise, block 4, role-rep). Config-overridable design space exhausted.

trial 1 cluster masking hurt (0.609, −0.028 — large contiguous holes remove too much sparse local signal). trial 2 cross-modal masking tied (0.635 — predicting density from atoms adds no affinity signal). trial 3 ligand masking (mask the ligand, predict it from the pocket — the de-novo task itself) running. So far dramatic masking/objective changes confirm the plateau; the most on-theme binding-aware ideas trial 3 (ligand 0.621) and trial 4 (interface 0.610) both HURT. Verdict: every targeted/concentrated masking hurts (cluster −0.028, interface −0.027, ligand −0.016); uniform scattered masking is optimal. Masking strategy is not the lever — the plateau is robust to it.

trial 6 (contrastive auxiliary) badly HURT — ρ 0.499, the WORST result in the campaign (−0.138). A SimCLR InfoNCE loss alongside MAE (two MAE-masked views pulled together, different complexes pushed apart) degraded val too (best_val 0.36 vs 0.53) — the features are worse, not mis-calibrated. Instance discrimination is anti-affinity: it pushes every different complex apart equally, discarding the graded similarity structure ρ needs (similar binders should sit close, not far). This was the last genuinely-different objective; both non-MAE objectives tried regress (denoise −0.050, contrastive −0.138), so reconstruction is actively protected and the plateau is firmly data/probe-ceiling bound, not objective-bound (the flat 6.5×-data result, Phase 4, agrees).

trial 7 (learnable radius-embed atoms) — the best dramatic trial, ρ 0.624. Replacing one-hot element channels with a learned φ(vdW-radius)→4 per role (a continuous, compressed atom featurization) beats roleblob 0.592 by +0.032 (it recovers the element separation role-occupancy sums away) and comes within −0.013 of one-hot atomblob 0.637 — a near-lossless substitute, not an improvement. The small gap is exactly the predicted cost: ligand S and P share vdW 1.8 Å, so radius cannot separate them. So even a learned atom featurization sits at the plateau; representation richness isn't the missing lever either.

Reading it

Density lifts ChannelViT over coords (best 0.641 vs coords-ChannelViT 0.604). The winning structure is group density+gradmag together, keep lig/poc separate ([7,4,2] > [11,2] > [7,4,1,1] > [11,1,1]).

The knob space is essentially flat. Across every trial, only grouping ever moved ρ. atom-bias (mask chemistry-rich voxels) is the lone nominal beat at ρ 0.641, but its ±.006 overlaps the [7,4,2] base (0.637) and its RMSE is worse (1.385 vs 1.353) — better ranking, worse calibration, i.e. within noise. Mask ratio (0.6/0.75), recon weight (0.3 tied, 1.0 worse), rope3d (0.610), wd0.1 (0.624) and HCS channel-group dropout (0.637/0.633) all tie-or-hurt.

Capacity is NOT the bottleneck. Doubling the encoder (dim768, 97.9M, 2.1×) regressed to ρ 0.626 (−0.011, worst RMSE) — a bigger model just overfits the frozen probe. So the plateau is data scale / probe-ceiling bound, not model size or pretext recipe. The masking-distribution axis is now closed too (mask 0.4 / block 4 hurt).
