VoxBind density-encoder pre-training - meeting summary

Hyun · 2026-05-26 · branch impl/electra
Headline. A 4.5M-param 3-D ViT-MAE pre-trained on synthetic electron density, then dropped frozen into VoxBind's density branch and trained on a 10× smaller data subset (10k vs 100k pocket crops), matches the full-data reproduction on Vina-dock within ±0.14 kcal/mol and effectively ties on high-affinity % (64.3 vs 65.0). Versus a non-pre-trained aligned-density baseline at the same 10k data scale, ViT-MAE buys -0.40 kcal/mol Vina-dock and +13.2 pts high-affinity on the 50-target overlap - bigger gain than adding the density branch itself.

1. What we did & why

VoxBind conditions a 3-D voxel diffusion model on a pocket density branch. Before this branch, density features were trained from scratch on a small downstream budget (10k pocket crops) and likely under-trained. We tested whether self-supervised pre-training on synthetic density can produce a strong encoder that downstream training only needs to fuse, not learn from scratch.

Three pre-training arms share the same synthetic-density pretext (sum-of-atom voxels → random Gaussian blur σ∈[0.30, 0.80]Å → per-sample z-score → additive noise). They differ only in encoder and objective:

ArmEncoderObjectiveWhy we ran it
CNN-MAE 4 × ResidualBlock (CNN) Block-mask + masked-voxel MSE + 11-ch atom-structure aux Shape-compatible drop-in for the existing CNN density encoder; cheapest baseline.
ViT-MAE 4.5M 3-D ViT (patch 8³, D=192, L=6, H=6) Same MAE pretext as CNN Apples-to-apples test of whether attention helps over CNN at fixed compute & pretext.
ViT-ELECTRA 4.5M 3-D ViT, identical config Block-corrupt (swap/reblur/noise) + per-patch RTD BCE + same aux Tests whether replaced-token-detection (more sample-efficient in NLP) beats MAE on dense density volumes.

All three encoders are shape-compatible drop-ins for VoxBind.density_encoder: same output shape, same fusion path, same downstream config. Only density_encoder_type and density_pretrained_path change.

Pre-training takeaways.
  • ViT-MAE reconstructs ~3.3× tighter than CNN-MAE at the same effective batch (val 0.062 vs 0.203). At matching ep55, ViT-MAE was already at 0.117 - so the gap is not just from CNN-MAE being killed.
  • ELECTRA solves its pretext too easily: val L_rtd collapses to 0.0025, meaning the discriminator detects swap/reblur/noise corruptions almost perfectly. The encoder gets little representation pressure, so the auxiliary structure head ends up doing the work but tops out worse than under MAE (L_str 0.276 vs 0.056).
  • Diagnosis: rule-based corruptions sit too far from the data manifold for a strong discriminator. The learned-generator path is implemented but not yet wired in.

Docking takeaways (effectiveness of what we did).
  1. The density branch alone (no SSL, aligned crops) buys -0.26 kcal/mol Vina-dock and +2.4 pts HA% over no-density baseline.
  2. Adding ViT-MAE pre-training on top of the density branch buys an additional -0.40 kcal/mol and +13.2 pts HA% - larger than the density branch's own contribution.
  3. Data efficiency: ViT-MAE on a 10× smaller training set lands within 0.14 kcal/mol Vina-dock of the full-data reproduction. Downstream looks feature-bound, not data-bound, at this scale.

3. Conclusions

  1. SSL pre-training on density works - and contributes more than just having the density branch. Headline positive result.
  2. ViT > CNN as the density encoder at this scale (4.5M params). ViT-MAE dominates CNN-MAE on pretext loss and downstream metrics.
  3. MAE > rule-based ELECTRA on this pretext. Swap/reblur/noise corruptions are too distinguishable - the discriminator solves the RTD task trivially and provides no representation pressure. Final ELECTRA downstream still pending but trajectory is clear.
  4. Downstream data efficiency: a frozen pre-trained ViT-MAE encoder + 10k subset reaches full-data reproduction quality. Cheaper to pre-train once than to scale downstream data.

4. Suggested next experiments (to grow the contribution)

  1. Scale-up: ViT-MAE 40M. The 42.6M config (D=512, L=12, H=8) is already written but not run. Tests whether the encoder is capacity-limited - current 4.5M is ViT-Tiny shaped. If MAE loss keeps dropping and downstream improves, we claim a scaling law for 3-D voxel SSL.
  2. Unfreeze + fine-tune the encoder. All current downstream runs use density_freeze=true. A short discriminative-LR fine-tune (encoder 1e-5, rest 1e-4, last 50 ep) usually buys another step. If it does not help here, that itself is a publishable finding.
  3. Real-density pretext. Current pretext is Gaussian-blurred atom voxels - a sanity-check distribution. Replace with real 2Fo-Fc maps from PDB or cryo-EM densities so the encoder learns features robust to true noise, resolution, and missing-density. This could be the headline contribution: "SSL transfers from real density → de novo ligand generation."
  4. Learned-generator ELECTRA. The cfg.electra.generator path is wired in but not enabled. A small CNN generator trained jointly produces distribution-matched corruptions and fixes the trivial-detection failure. Worth one run before declaring ELECTRA dead.
  5. Bigger pretext corpus. Pretext is synthetic from CrossDocked atoms only. Adding PDB-mined pockets (no x-ray needed) easily 10×'s the data - often the unlock for ViT scaling.
  6. Cross-modal contrastive pretext. Replace the structure-MSE aux head with an InfoNCE objective between density-encoder output and a separate ligand-atom encoder. Directly targets the cross-modal alignment we ultimately need for diffusion conditioning.
  7. Linear-probing study. Probe what the pre-trained encoder has actually learned: (a) atom-type classification, (b) secondary-structure recovery, (c) ligand-pose RMSD prediction. Gives interpretable mechanisms for the downstream Vina deltas - turns "it works" into "we explain why."
  8. Fulltest sampling+docking for CNN-MAE and ViT-ELECTRA. Immediate next step before any paper writeup - currently only ViT-MAE has 79-target Vina numbers.

Appendix B · Pretext objective details

Shared input synthesis

density = sum_over_channels(voxels_lig) + sum_over_channels(voxels_poc)
        → GaussianBlur3D(sigma ~ U[0.30, 0.80] Aa)
        → per-sample z-score
        → + N(0, 0.05^2)         # additive Gaussian noise

MAE pretext (CNN-MAE, ViT-MAE)

  • 3-D block mask at 8³ voxel blocks, mask_ratio = 0.60. Masked voxels are zeroed in the encoder input.
  • L = L_dens + L_str
    • L_dens = MSE(d_hat, d_clean) restricted to masked voxels.
    • L_str = pos-weighted MSE on 11-ch atom voxels. Voxels with target > 0.05 get 100× weight to counter the ~99.8%-sparse target (without this, the network learns the constant-zero shortcut).

ELECTRA pretext (ViT-ELECTRA)

  • Same block mask, but masked blocks are replaced per batch by one of:
    • swap (60%) - copy same region from a neighbour-shifted sample
    • reblur (30%) - re-blur the same atoms with a different sigma
    • noise (10%) - N(0,1) at the z-scored scale
  • Per-patch BCE-with-logits on RTD head over the 8³=512-patch grid. Target = 1 if any voxel in the patch was corrupted.
  • Structure aux head retained, identical loss as MAE.

Appendix E · Open items / known limitations

  • CNN-MAE pre-training was killed at ep55 to free GPUs - final pretext loss is "best we have" not "best possible". Re-running to 100 ep would tighten the apples-to-apples claim.
  • ViT-ELECTRA downstream still has ~43 epochs left as of 2026-05-26. Final mIoU may close some gap but trajectory clearly underperforms ViT-MAE.
  • Only ViT-MAE has a fulltest sampling+docking run (79 targets). Matching runs for CNN-MAE and ViT-ELECTRA are needed before any paper writeup.
  • 40M ViT config is committed but not yet launched.
  • Vina scoring has a few clash-explosion outliers (vina_score >= 0 on ~2 targets out of 79) - median Vina-dock is more robust than mean. ViT-MAE median Vina-dock = -8.06 vs reproduction -8.12; deltas hold.
