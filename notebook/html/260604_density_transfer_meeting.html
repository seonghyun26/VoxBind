Does experimental density transfer to protein–drug tasks?

Hyun · research meeting 2026-06-04 · covers experiments 2026-05-17 → 2026-06-04 · branch main
Question. Electron-density inputs have helped in protein–protein structure work and on regression read-outs. Does that benefit carry over to the protein–drug setting, on both a regression task (binding-affinity prediction) and a generative task (de novo ligand design)?
Answer (so far): transfer is partial and asymmetric. On the generative task density is a conditional win — the model demonstrably uses real density (beats a structureless-noise control) and, once self-supervised pre-training + crop alignment are added, the density branch buys −0.67 kcal/mol Vina-dock and +15.7 pts high-affinity at 10× less data conditional yes. On the regression task density does not help at all — a coords-only atom encoder (ρ = 0.473) beats every density-fed variant, and feeding experimental density at full strength actively degrades the representation (ρ down to 0.26) no. Working interpretation (hypothesis, not yet proven — see next-step #4): density may act as a spatial prior for where atoms go (helping generation) while carrying little binding-affinity signal beyond atom coordinates (failing regression). Both checks are scored on docking/affinity read-outs, so this placement-vs-affinity split is a conjecture to test, not a measured result.

1. Hypothesis & roadmap

Prior evidence establishes density as a useful signal along two separate axes — in protein–protein structural modelling, and on regression read-outs generally. VoxBind sits in the unexplored corner: protein–drug. We test that corner on both task types.

*v3 (max-abs) is the only density variant to reach the plain-atomblob level — it ties it on Spearman (0.422 vs 0.421) and slightly edges it on Pearson (test r 0.461 vs 0.452), but still trails the best coords encoder atomblob_weighted (r 0.511) on every metric. The ranking is monotonic in density "loudness": the quieter the density channel, the closer the encoder reverts to the atom-only baseline. v3 effectively mutes density rather than representing it.

Check 1 verdict density does NOT help regression. No density-fed encoder beats the best coords-only encoder on any metric: atomblob_weighted (ρ 0.473 / test r 0.511 / val r 0.558) leads everything. The best density variant (v3) only reaches the plain-atomblob level — and only by nearly disabling its own density input. When density is given real training weight (v1/v2/v4) the representation gets worse, not better — consistently across both Spearman and Pearson, val and test. Experimental density adds no usable affinity signal on top of atom coordinates.

2.2  Check 2 — Protein–drug × generative (VoxBind generation + Vina docking)

Setup. VoxBind is a 3-D voxel-diffusion ligand generator conditioned on a pocket density branch. We ran two complementary tests. (a) Content A/B: identical architecture & data, swapping the density channel between real X-ray density, a structureless smoothed-noise control (density-like texture, no ligand signal), and no density branch — isolates whether the model uses density content. Check 2 verdict density helps generation, conditionally. The generator uses real density (beats the noise control), and with SSL pre-training + alignment the density branch becomes a clear data-efficiency lever: −0.67 kcal/mol and +15.7 HA% over no-density at the same 10k budget. The caveat: raw density-conditioning (no SSL) only matches the no-density baseline, and full-data coords-only is still strongest on Vina-dock energy (−7.99 vs −7.85) — so the gain is mostly in data efficiency, not a new dock-energy ceiling (though SSL density does edge full-data on high-affinity %).

3. Conclusions

  1. Transfer is asymmetric across task type. The same experimental-density signal that fails the protein–drug regression task helps the protein–drug generative task. A plausible (but not-yet-tested) explanation: density informs where atoms sit — a spatial prior the generator can use — more than it adds to affinity prediction beyond atom coordinates. This placement-vs-affinity story is the hypothesis behind next-step #4, not a measured conclusion.
  2. Regression: no. Coords-only encoders dominate (ρ 0.473); every density-fed encoder is equal-or-worse, and "louder" density monotonically degrades the representation. The one tie (v3) is an artifact of muting density, not of representing it.
  3. Generative: conditional yes. Density content is demonstrably used (beats noise), and SSL pre-training converts the density branch into a real data-efficiency win (−0.67 kcal/mol, +15.7 HA% at 10× less data) — but it needs SSL + alignment to pay off, and does not beat full-data coords-only on dock energy (it does edge it on high-affinity %).
  4. Net. Density's protein–protein / regression track record does not cleanly generalise to protein–drug. It generalises to generative conditioning (partially, fragilely) and not to affinity regression.

4. Next experiments

  1. Fair regression re-test. The current ranking is confounded by density loudness (scale = hidden loss weight). Equalise the density-channel input variance (or add per-channel loss normalisation) so normalisation shape is compared fairly — and probe the still-pending plain-v4 control to separate "dual-head decoder" from "v4 norm." If a fairly-weighted density rep still can't beat coords, the regression verdict is firm.
  2. Real density, not synthetic. Pre-training density is currently Gaussian-blurred atom voxels. Swap in real 2Fo–Fc maps (PDB-REDO MTZ → gemmi FFT, already validated as a drop-in) so the encoder sees true noise / resolution / missing density — the regime where density is supposed to add information.
  3. Close the generative loop. Confirm the SSL-density gain on the full 79-pocket aligned re-sample, and test whether SSL-density can beat full-data coords-only — i.e. does density raise the ceiling or only the efficiency?
  4. Explain the dissociation. Linear-probe the density encoder for atom-type recovery vs. pose/affinity-correlated features to mechanistically explain why density helps placement but not affinity.

Appendix A · Provenance & methods

Check 1 — PDBbind affinity probe

  • Encoders: 40M-param 3-D ViT-MAE pre-trained on CrossDocked X-ray-available subset (n=78,428). Atom-only arms 260528_atomblob_vit_mae_40m_weighted (+ atom-biased mask, inv-sqrt-freq channel weights) and plain atomblob; density arms atomblob_(merged_)density with normalisations v1–v4 (per-map z+±3σ clip / pool z-score / pool max-abs / pool clip+z).
  • Probe: frozen encoder → pocket feature (dim 512) → 2-layer MLP, early-stopped on val Spearman, 3 seeds. Code dataset/01c_pdbbind_probe.py; results dataset/data/pdbbind/probe_results_e99_*.csv; logs log/01f_probe_*.log.
  • Mechanism note verified in code: density fed to MAE un-normalised (train_density_vit_mae.py), reconstructed with plain MSE (compute_losses) ⇒ recon-loss weight ∝ (density scale)²; v3 = v2 × (σpool/max_abs) ≈ v2 × 0.011.

Check 2 — VoxBind generation + docking

  • Content A/B: 260515_voxbind_100ep_density / ..._noise / 260514_..._baseline (pilot, 100-pocket subset, sampled on 11 val pockets, dock-eval log/260517_dock_eval_density_ab.log); 10k arms 260518_voxbind_10k_{density,noise} (log/260519_dock_eval_10k_test_ab.log).
  • Noise control = smoothed Gaussian-noise crops, density-like texture (corr length ~5.4 vox), no ligand signal (dataset/00c_make_noise_density.py).
  • SSL capability test: aligned-density branch 260519_voxbind_10k_density_aligned (ep138) and ViT-MAE-pre-trained branch (260523_voxbind_10k_density_vit_mae_frozen, ep379); overlap-only target means; see the 2026-05-26 pre-training meeting summary for the full Vina table.
  • Generation & evaluation are decoupled: sample.py generates SDFs; standalone notebook/webapp/metrics.py docks (AutoDock Vina, exhaustiveness 8) and writes per-target metrics.json. High-affinity % = fraction of generated ligands docking stronger than the reference.

Caveats

  • Docking means use small/medium target sets (11 pilot, 50–79 at 10k) and a few clash-explosion outliers; medians track the means but magnitudes are noisy at this n.
  • The 10k content A/B reports density vs. noise only (the 10k no-density baseline finished later and is only partially dock-evaluated); the cleanest density-vs-baseline comparison is the pilot row.
  • Probe numbers are on a frozen encoder + shallow head — a lower bound on extractable affinity signal, not a tuned affinity model.
