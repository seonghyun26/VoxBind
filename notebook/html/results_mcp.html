Macrocyclic peptide generation — receptor-ED conditioning

Does conditioning on the receptor's deposited electron density help generation on a second modality? This is the same question the CrossDocked work asks, moved to macrocyclic peptides: FuncBind's MCP arm, vanilla against a receptor-ED ControlNet fine-tune, on the held-out MCP test split.

Not comparable to the CrossDocked tables in results_drug_design.html. These ligands are 45–55 heavy atoms against ~24 there, the targets are a different split, and QED/SA are calibrated on small-molecule drugs — a 50-atom cyclic peptide scores low on them by construction, not by failure. Read the two documents side by side, never as one table.

One of the ten targets (3dv5) produced nothing from either model and is excluded, so averages are over 9 targets. PoseCheck for macrocycles has not been run — the protein-preparation step is the same, but nothing in this project has validated PoseCheck's strain calculation on 50-atom cyclic peptides, so those cells stay empty rather than being filled with unvalidated numbers.

On this budget the fine-tune loses on both axes. It produced 118 molecules against vanilla's 213 from an identical budget — filling the 25-molecule quota on 3 of 10 targets versus 7 — and its Vina Dock is 1.65 kcal/mol worse. An earlier partial read at 21–28 molecules had the two tied; that was a small-sample artefact and the full run reversed it, which is the reason the partial numbers are not quoted here.

AAppendix — what is still missing

  • Full-budget run. The pilot used 256 chains / 1 attempt against the paper's 756 chains / 10 attempts, so absolute yields here are far below what the method can produce. Both models shared the budget exactly, which is what makes the comparison usable despite that.
  • PoseCheck. Protein preparation is identical to the small-molecule path, but nothing in this project has checked that PoseCheck's UFF strain calculation behaves sensibly on 50-atom cyclic peptides. Those cells stay empty rather than carrying unvalidated numbers.
  • Rigid-fragment consistency. Peptide backbones are almost entirely rotatable bonds, so the rigid fragments the metric relies on are mostly single residues — whether the measure means the same thing here as it does for fused-ring small molecules is an open question, not a given.
