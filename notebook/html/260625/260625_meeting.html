4 Encoding more atoms

Table 5  ·  Role-split input — encoding atom identity by blob size, not by channel

The standard encoder uses one voxel channel per element type (7 ligand + 4 pocket = 11 atom channels). The role-split representation collapses these into just 2 “role” channels — ligand-occupancy and pocket-occupancy — and lets the van-der-Waals blob size carry atom identity (element-wise vdW radius, ligand_radius=−1). The motivation: free the encoder from a fixed element vocabulary so it can ingest arbitrary heavy elements (metals, Se, halogens) that the 7/4 vocab drops — “encoding more atoms”. Two variants are compared against the coords-only (C) and per-element full (C + D + G) ChannelViT baselines on lp_edrscc_v2 (Kd/Ki, 3850 / 817 / 1320, mean ± std over 3 seeds): role-split sums the same 7/4 heavy-atom vocab into the 2 role channels (Phase 1), and role-split + diverse keeps every heavy element with its own per-atom vdW radius (Phase 2 — pretrained on the diverse PLINDER corpus of 17,530 complexes).

The loss is larger than the entire density+gradmag gain (+0.026), so a 2-channel blob-size encoding more than erases the benefit of adding D+G.
  • Why. The per-element channel decomposition carries affinity-relevant atom-identity signal that vdW blob size in 2 role channels cannot recover. Tiny seed std (±0.002) → this is a robust effect, not noise.
  • Diverse atoms recover part of the gap — but role-split still loses. Keeping every heavy element (metals / Se / halogens) with its own vdW radius lifts role-split from 0.554 → 0.585 (+0.031), so the previously-dropped atoms do carry real affinity signal. Yet diverse role-split still trails coords-only (−0.019) and the per-element full model (−0.045): encoding more atom types helps, but the 2-channel blob-size representation remains worse than an explicit per-element channel decomposition. Pretrained on the diverse PLINDER corpus (17,530 complexes), frozen probe, 3 seeds.

A Appendix — dataset filtering & splits

Provenance for every data count in this document. The encoder is self-supervised-pretrained on a PLINDER ligand-matched X-ray density corpus (§A.1); the frozen encoder is then probed for binding affinity on LP-PDBBind under two splits (§A.2). The two pools are disjoint by construction — PLINDER selection drops every PDB entry that appears in any downstream test/val set. Counts are the exact on-disk numbers (sources: plinder_funnel.json, ligand_rscc.csv, ed_rscc_split.csv, voxbind/splits/lp_edrscc_v1.csv).

The raw LP-PDBBind new_split (IC50-inclusive, locally-availability-dependent, modality arms saw different pools) is no longer reported — superseded by the frozen, density-matched, Kd/Ki-only lp_edrscc_v2 above.

  • Why Kd/Ki-only (v2). The old IC50-inclusive pool (10,128) was 53% IC50 in the test set; IC50 is assay-dependent (substrate/enzyme concentration, mechanism), so pIC50 is not comparable to the thermodynamic pKd/pKi. Dropping IC50 leaves 5,987 with a consistent target. On it, both arms score the identical 1,320 test complexes (density available for all), so any density effect is a clean modality attribution.
  • Effect on the density claim. Removing IC50 lifts every encoder, but the matched density gain shrinks from +0.025 to +0.003 ρ (C 0.605 → C+D+G 0.608): coords benefit most, so much of the apparent v1 gain was density absorbing IC50 label-noise, not binding signal. ChannelViT still extracts a real margin ([7,4,2] 0.637, +0.032 over coords) — see Table 2.
  • ED-availability is the dominant filter cut (19,443 → 12,674), larger than RSCC ≥ 0.8 (11,601 → 10,926), non-covalent, or the Kd/Ki restriction. The final 5,987 are partitioned by LP-PDBBind's sequence-clustered new_split, so the probe measures novel-target generalization.
